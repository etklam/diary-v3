# [118] Give navigation a pending state

Status: accepted
Execution: done 2026-10-15
Published: 2026-10-15

Category: bug
Type: Cross-cutting interaction defect
User stories covered: Cross-cutting navigation — every route change in the private workspace.

## Observation

**`useNavigation()` appears zero times in `apps/web/app`.**

```
$ grep -rn "useNavigation()" apps/web/app | wc -l
0
```

This is a React Router SSR app whose loaders call the API. Between the click on a sidebar
destination and the first paint of the next route, the browser keeps the *old* page on
screen and nothing anywhere indicates that work started. There is no top progress
indicator, no optimistic active state on the clicked item, and no `viewTransition` on any
`<Link>` (`grep -rn "viewTransition\|startViewTransition" apps/web/app` → 0).

The app is not short of loading vocabulary — it has a real `Skeleton` component
(`ui.tsx:80`, `styles.css:524-541`) and `FailureNotice` + retry is consistent across async
surfaces. The gap is specific: **client-side route transitions are the one async operation
with no feedback at all.** Everything else has some.

A second, smaller defect sits in the same area. The in-flight pattern on submit buttons is
a label swap — `busy ? t('pending') : c.save` — used in `stock-notes.tsx:75`,
`evidence.tsx:200`, `review-reschedule.tsx:16`, `routes/diary.tsx:160` and others. Swapping
the label changes the button's width mid-submit, so the action row reflows at exactly the
moment the user is watching it.

Measured for context: the whole app ships **24 `transition:` declarations and 1
`@keyframes`** across every stylesheet. Motion here is absent rather than deliberately
restrained, and `DESIGN.md` already defines `--dur-1/2/3` and `--ease-out` that almost
nothing uses.

## Requested outcome

Every navigation gives feedback within one frame of the click, and no in-flight control
changes size. The treatment must honour `prefers-reduced-motion` and must not introduce a
layout shift of its own.

## Implementation plan

**1. A global pending indicator in `root.tsx`.** Drive it from `useNavigation().state`
(`idle` / `loading` / `submitting`). A top rule that grows across the content region is
consistent with the ledger system and costs no layout — it must be `position: fixed` and
painted over the header, not inserted into the flow. Only show it after a short delay
(~120ms, `--dur-1`) so fast loads do not flash.

**2. Optimistic active state on the clicked destination.** `useNavigation().location`
gives the pending pathname; the sidebar item for it should take the selection treatment
immediately rather than waiting for `aria-current` to move after the loader resolves.
Keep `aria-current="page"` on the *resolved* route so assistive technology is not told the
page changed before it did; announce the pending state separately.

**3. Announce route changes.** Add one polite live region that names the destination once
navigation settles. Today a screen-reader user gets no event at all on a client-side
route change — this is the accessibility half of the same defect.

**4. Fix the label-swap reflow.** Either reserve the width (`min-width` from the longer of
the two strings) or keep the label and show the pending state as a leading mark, and apply
it through one shared control rather than per call site. Audit the ~17 call sites that use
`t('pending')`.

**5. Consider `<Link viewTransition>` for same-shell navigations** once 1–3 are in. It is
the cheapest way to make the content swap read as one movement instead of a repaint, and
React Router supports it directly. It is step 5 and not step 1 because it is the
decorative part; the information gap is steps 1–3.

**Verification:** `workspace-navigation.spec.ts` and `narrow-viewport.spec.ts` must pass.
Add a case that throttles a loader and asserts the indicator is present during
`state === 'loading'` and absent at `idle`, and a case asserting the live region receives
the destination name. Add a `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` assertion that the indicator
appears without animating.

## Execution record — 2026-10-15

Steps 1–4 shipped. Step 5 (`<Link viewTransition>`) was **not taken**, and the reason is
step 1's own finding: see ruling 2.

**New and changed:** `navigation-progress.tsx` (`NavigationProgress`,
`usePendingPathname`), `ActionButton` in `ui.tsx`, the in-flight block in `styles.css`, the
`data-nav-pending` treatment on the three navigation lists, and the live region in
`root.tsx`. 23 files took the `ActionButton` conversion.

**Ruling 1 — the live region does not take `role="status"`.** It was written that way
first and `account-security.spec.ts` immediately failed on a strict-mode violation:
`getByRole('status')` resolved to two elements on a page that already had one. The
collision is not a test artifact — most pages in this product own a status landmark for
their own save and refresh states, and adding a second to *every* page makes the phrase
"the status region" ambiguous product-wide. `aria-live="polite"` plus `aria-atomic` on a
plain element announces identically without claiming the role.

**Ruling 2 — the premise "its loaders call the API" holds for 8 routes, not 90.**
`grep` over `routes/` finds a loader in `articles`, `article`, `login`, `forgot-password`,
`reset-password`, `discipline-share`, and the two blog redirects. Every private workspace
route fetches client-side from the component, so `useNavigation()` on those routes reports
`loading` only while the route module itself is being fetched. That changes what each step
is worth, and is why the work landed in this order:

- **Step 2 is the one that answers the click**, not step 1. It does not depend on a loader:
  the clicked item takes the selection fill the moment the navigation starts, and for a
  loader-less route whose module is already loaded the navigation completes in the same
  frame — so the feedback is immediate either way.
- **Step 1 covers the genuine gap**, which is the first visit to a route whose module has
  not been fetched yet, plus every loader-bearing route.
- **Step 5 would have been decoration over a problem that is already answered** by those
  two, and it is the one step that affects every route's paint path. Left undone
  deliberately rather than silently.

**Ruling 3 — the in-flight mark is a rule, not a leading glyph.** The ticket offered
`min-width` from the longer string or a leading mark. A reserved `min-width` has to be
computed per locale and silently widens every idle control; a leading mark inside the flow
is itself a width change. What shipped keeps the label untouched and paints a 2px sweep
along the control's bottom edge — absolutely positioned, so the width provably cannot
change. It is the same mark as the route indicator at a different scale, which gives the
product one vocabulary for "this has started and has not finished" instead of two.

**Verification.** `tests/e2e/navigation-feedback.spec.ts`, 4/4 green: the indicator
appears and is `position: fixed` with the content region unmoved at both edges of its life
(measured inside one shell, since the first navigation legitimately changes chrome); a fast
navigation is sampled 12 times across its whole life and never shows it; the submit control
is sampled by a `MutationObserver` inside the page rather than polled from outside, so a
width that changed for one frame would still be caught; and under
`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the bar is present, full width, `animation-name: none`.
`account-security` 3/3, `workspace-navigation`, `narrow-viewport`, `watchlist`,
`discipline`, `partners`, `diary-review`, `trade-plan`, `api-keys`, `achievements`,
`evidence` all green. `tsc --noEmit`, `eslint`, `design:check` and 1190 unit tests pass.

**A pre-existing failure was found here and fixed.** Both `alerts.spec.ts` cases failed, at
1440 and at 390, waiting on the Diary reminders link. Confirmed not to be this work by
stashing it and rerunning: 2 failed, 7 passed on clean `HEAD`. The cause was a stale
assertion in the harness, not the product — `clickNav` in `tests/support/e2e.ts` carried
the comment "Only the trade-management group is still a disclosure; diary sub-items sit in
the open list", which `nav.tsx` has not matched since Diary management became a `nav-more`
disclosure holding AI reports, Diary reminders and Partners. `Diary reminders` and
`Partners` joined the helper's `secondary` list; `alerts` and `price-alerts` are 9/9.
