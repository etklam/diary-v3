# [120] Decide whether the system has more than one density

Status: accepted
Execution: done 2026-10-15
Published: 2026-10-15

Category: enhancement
Type: Design decision
User stories covered: Cross-cutting — every surface in the product.

## Observation

**`DESIGN.md` defines one texture and the app applies it everywhere.** Hairline rule,
label-above-value, sentence-case heading, the same `--text-*` scale and the same
`--space-5` rhythm, on capture surfaces, on data tables and on reading surfaces alike.

That uniformity is why the page-score programme succeeded: tickets 101–116 drove 69 of 84
route states to zero mechanical findings, and `ui-page-score-2026-10-09.md` scores
`/timeline` at 87 and `/` at 81. **It is also, now, the product's most visible remaining
weakness, and the existing rubric cannot see it** — the rubric measures conformance to a
single texture, so a surface that should not have that texture scores well for having it.

Three measured consequences, from the 2026-10-09 captures:

- **`/diaries/quick` — the core capture surface — wastes the canvas it is given.** A
  690×280 textarea sits in the top-left of a 1,225px content region, with the right rail's
  three disclosures ending around y=330 and roughly 550px of nothing below. The most
  important writing surface in the product is the one with the least considered
  composition.
- **`/diaries/:id` at 390 runs 2,984px with every section heading at the same rank.**
  "Guru context at decision time" — a secondary, optional module — sits at the same visual
  weight as "Transactions". There is no h2/h3 split doing work, so nothing recedes.
- **`/stocks` interleaves four full-width prose sentences between the label/value pairs of
  its valuation grid**, so a scan intended for figures breaks four times. (The copy itself
  is [121](121-figure-caveat-mechanism.md); the interleaving is a density problem.)

The common cause is that a table and an essay are being asked to breathe the same way.
Paper — the system's own metaphor — does not do that: a ledger page is dense and a reading
page has margins, and they are recognisably the same book.

## Requested outcome

A ruling on whether "The Ledger" has one register or several, recorded in `DESIGN.md`, with
every route assigned to one. If the answer is one, that should be argued rather than left
as the default, and these three surfaces need a different fix.

## Implementation plan

**1. Decide the registers.** The candidate set, which is a starting point and not the
answer:

- **Capture** — writing surfaces (`/diaries/quick`, `/diaries/new`, the review form). Wide
  measure, generous leading, few controls visible at once, the field is the subject of the
  page.
- **Ledger** — tables and figure grids (`/stocks`, `/trade-plans`, the tools). Dense rows,
  tight leading, the current texture, essentially unchanged.
- **Reading** — entries and articles (`/diaries/:id`, `/articles/:slug`). Capped measure,
  a real heading hierarchy, secondary modules visibly subordinate.

Each register needs its own measure, spacing step and heading scale — not its own colours,
not its own components. The vocabulary stays single; the rhythm does not.

**2. Argue the alternative honestly.** "One register, deliberately" is a defensible answer
for a tool whose virtue is that it disappears into the task, and the product register's own
guidance says consistency beats surprise. If that is the ruling, record it and fix the three
surfaces above as individual composition defects instead.

**3. Assign every route.** A table in `DESIGN.md`, in the same form as the existing
navigation recipe. Routes that resist assignment are the interesting cases and should be
named.

**4. Add the check.** `scripts/check-design-contrast.mjs` is the precedent: a rule that is
only in prose drifts. Whatever the ruling, the density assignment needs a check beside it
or it will decay exactly the way the Figure Rule did (72 blocks adrift before anyone
measured).

**Verification:** no behaviour changes, so the gate is the visual evidence. Re-capture the
affected routes at 390/768/1440 before and after, and record them under `docs/design/`.

## Ruling — three registers, 2026-10-15

**The decisive argument was already in `DESIGN.md`.** Principle 5 — "Reading and working
density stay separate" — has been in force on paper since the beginning and was never
operative anywhere in the app. The ticket frames this as a choice between one register and
several; it is closer to a choice between *enforcing the rule the system already states*
and deleting it. Enforcing it is the ruling, with the third register — capture — added
because the product's own core surface is a writing surface and neither of the two existing
halves of principle 5 describes it.

"One register, deliberately" was argued and rejected **on the evidence, not on taste**, and
the argument is recorded in `DESIGN.md` beside the ruling so the next reader gets both. In
short: one texture is cheap to hold and had already produced the three measured failures
this ticket lists; each is a composition defect only if you accept that every surface
should breathe the same way, and a rhythm defect otherwise — in which case one ruling fixes
all three and the next one too.

## Execution record — 2026-10-15

**The mechanism.** `apps/web/app/registers.ts` is the single table: every route path
exactly as `routes.ts` writes it, mapped to `capture` / `ledger` / `reading` / `none`.
`registerFor(pathname)` resolves it at runtime by longest-literal match, so `/diaries/new`
beats `/diaries/:id` without the table depending on its own order. `root.tsx` writes the
answer to `data-register` on `main`, and `styles.css` hangs `--register-measure` and
`--register-rhythm` off it. 88 routes: 15 capture, 56 ledger, 13 reading, 4 not a page.

**Ruling 1 — the ledger register changes nothing, and that is the point.** It is the
texture 56 of 88 routes already had. Declaring it is what makes the other two *departures*
rather than exceptions, and it is what stops the next surface defaulting into a rhythm
nobody chose. A register system in which every register is new would have been a rewrite,
not a ruling.

**Ruling 2 — the check is of assignment, not of drift, and the ticket allows this
explicitly.** `scripts/check-register-assignment.mjs` fails when `routes.ts` and
`registers.ts` disagree in either direction. It cannot check that a page *renders* in its
register, because a register is a measure, a rhythm and a heading step — properties of
rendered layout, not of source text, and only measurable against a browser. What is
statically checkable is also what actually decays: a new route arriving with no register at
all, which is precisely how the single-texture default reasserted itself for 88 routes
before this existed. `tests/e2e/registers.spec.ts` carries the rendered half, one case per
register. Wired into `npm run design:check`.

**Ruling 3 — `/diaries/:id`'s hierarchy is a grouping, not a demotion of every module.**
The ticket asked for an h2/h3 split. `Evidence` and `DiaryGuruSnapshots` render their own
`h2` and are reused on `/stocks/:symbol`, where that level is correct; threading a heading
level through both to serve one page would have put a layout concern inside two components
that do not have one. What shipped instead: the aside becomes a labelled region with its
own heading — "Attached to this entry" — and the reading register sets everything inside it
one step down. The page gains the second-level grouping it did not have, every module reads
as inside it, and the two components stay level-agnostic. Recorded because it is a weaker
reading of "h2/h3 split" than the ticket's, chosen deliberately.

**`/diaries/quick`.** The writing area was `clamp(220px, 32dvh, 420px)` — 288px at a 900px
viewport, which is the 690×280 field the ticket measured. On the full page it is now
`clamp(280px, calc(100dvh - 420px), 720px)`, so the field takes the canvas the page is
given; the Cmd/J dialog keeps the old clamp, because it is already bounded by the viewport
and a taller floor there would make the dialog scroll. The e2e case pins both halves: the
field is over 400px tall at 1440×900 **and** still under 900px wide, because the writing
column carries the 72ch measure and widening the field would have traded one defect for
another.

**Evidence.** `docs/design/evidence/registers/` holds `capture-1440.png`,
`reading-1440.png`, `reading-768.png` and `reading-390.png`, captured by the spec itself so
they cannot go stale silently. The before state is the measurement in this ticket's own
Observation and the 2026-10-09 page-score captures it was taken from; no separate before
run was staged, since reverting the tree to photograph it would have produced the same
numbers the Observation already records.

**Verification.** `registers` 3/3, and no regression in the surfaces the registers touch:
`quick-authoring-follow-up` 15/15, `full-authoring-follow-up` 9/9, `diary-editor`,
`diary-detail-review`, `portfolio` 4/4, `overview` 4/4, `daily-workspace` 4/4. `tsc`,
`eslint`, and all four `design:check` scripts pass.
