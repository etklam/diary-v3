# [99] Redesign the Diary calendar around density and legible destinations

Status: needs-triage
Execution: todo
Published: 2026-10-04

Category: enhancement
Type: Design follow-up
User stories covered: US-021 Calendar navigation. Diary discovery.

## Observation

A design review of `/calendar` (`apps/web/app/routes/calendar.tsx`, `apps/web/app/calendar.css`, `apps/web/app/calendar-copy.ts`) scored 24/40 against Nielsen's heuristics. The deterministic slop detector returned zero findings. The domain layer is sound — month keys, heatmap weeks, coverage and the US equity calendar all live in `@diary/domain` with honest "not computed" states when a year is unsupported, and the hatched holiday treatment is one of the patterns DESIGN.md holds up as correct.

The problems are density, notation and destination legibility.

**Six rows of chrome stand between the heading and the grid.** `calendar.tsx:165-180` renders h1, lede, a standalone `Your timezone: …` prose line, the month control bar, an h2 repeating the month the control already shows, a summary line, and a holiday-policy line. The month is stated twice, in two different type sizes, about 120px apart.

**The grid is enormous and nearly empty.** `.calendar-grid` is `repeat(7, minmax(0, 1fr))` with `min-height: 100px` per cell (74px below the breakpoint), stretched across the full 1280px content width. That is roughly 180×100px of surface per cell to carry a two-digit number. A month is a dense object; this renders it as a sparse table.

**Three different destinations hide behind identical-looking cells.** `open()` (`calendar.tsx:130`) sends a day with a diary to `/diaries/:id`, a day with only trades or reviews to the merged timeline, and an empty day to `/diaries/quick?date=`. All three cells look the same and announce nothing about which. The empty-day case is the most valuable — it is a capture entry point — and it is the least visible.

**Two of the five cell marks have no visible key.** Cells carry `●` for a diary, `N ↔` for trades and `N ✓` for reviews (`calendar.tsx:183`), and the legend (`:190`) explains only `●`, `○` and `/`. A sighted reader gets no key for `↔` or `✓`; the information exists only in `aria-label`. Those glyphs are also raw typography where DESIGN.md specifies the single `icons.tsx` family, as are the `‹` and `›` month buttons.

**The year view scrolls only because its cells are 2.5× too large.** `.heatmap-week` sets `grid-template-rows: repeat(7, 28px)` with a `space-1` gap, so 53 weeks occupy roughly 1700px and the region must scroll horizontally — inside a page that already scrolls, which on touch means nested scrolling. There are no month labels on the axis, so a cell's position carries no readable time reference. At 13–14px per cell a full year fits in about 900px and needs no scroll at desktop at all, which is the entire point of a year view.

**It is not a heatmap.** `cell.level ? 'recorded' : ''` is binary presence. The heading is "Past 371 days" — a machine-derived 53×7 surfaced as user-facing copy — and the legend offers Recorded / No diary. Either give the cells real levels from the `transactionCount` and `reviewCount` already in the response, or stop calling it a heatmap and name it for what it is.

**Two keyboard models on one page.** The heatmap implements roving tabindex correctly (`tabIndex={cell.dateKey === today ? 0 : -1}`, arrows move focus). The month grid does not: every one of up to 31 day buttons is in the tab order, so tabbing past the calendar costs 31 stops, and the arrow-key handler indexes into `querySelectorAll('button')` instead.

**The year range is refetched on every month change.** The effect at `calendar.tsx:72` depends on `[month, today, attempt]` and issues both requests together, but the second covers `today - 370 → today` and does not depend on `month` at all. Paging through twelve months issues twelve redundant year-range queries.

**Coverage is an undefined number presented in bold.** `Monthly coverage: 3%` names no denominator. The reader cannot tell whether 3% is of calendar days, trading days, or days since the month began, and no action is attached to it.

**Minor.** The empty-month message is a muted `<p>` placed after the grid rather than `.empty-state` inside it. The timezone line is prose where it should be a footnote on the control bar. The recorded captures in `docs/design/evidence/calendar/` predate the current visual system — they show the old green `diary-v3` brand and the flat pre-grouping navigation — so the acceptance evidence for this page no longer represents it.

## Requested outcome

Rebuild the calendar as a dense, scannable month that makes each day's destination legible, and turn the year strip into something that can actually be read at a glance. Preserve the domain logic, the holiday semantics, the honest unsupported-year states, and every existing destination.

## Implementation plan

Delivered as one change set, in this order.

**1. Collapse the chrome above the grid.**
- One header (h1 + lede), then one control bar carrying the month input, `‹ Today ›`, and the month's figures as a ledger line — recorded days, coverage, holiday policy — instead of three separate paragraphs.
- Drop the h2 that repeats the month shown in the control.
- The timezone becomes a quiet footnote on the control bar, not a standalone prose line.

**2. Fix grid density.**
- Cap cell height and bound the grid width so a month reads as one object rather than stretching to 1280px; the page keeps its data-page container.
- Replace `●`, `↔`, `✓`, `‹` and `›` with marks from `icons.tsx`, and extend the legend to cover every mark the grid can show.

**3. Make the three destinations legible.**
- A day holding a diary, a day holding only trades or reviews, and an empty day get visibly distinct treatments, and each announces what opening it will do.
- The empty-day capture affordance becomes visible on hover and focus rather than being an invisible behavior of an identical-looking cell.

**4. Make the year view a year view.**
- Reduce cell size so 53 weeks fit without horizontal scroll at desktop; keep the scroll region only below the width where it cannot fit, and keep it keyboard-focusable per the DESIGN.md scroll-region rule.
- Add month labels along the axis.
- Rename "Past 371 days" to a human range.
- Derive real levels from the diary, transaction and review counts already present in `DiaryActivityDay` — ruled 2026-10-04 — with a legend stating what each level means, at most four steps, and a ramp held clear of the market palette so a busy day never reads as a gain or a loss.

**5. One keyboard model.**
- The month grid adopts the heatmap's roving tabindex: a single tab stop, arrows move within the grid, Home/End keep their current month-bounded behavior. The arrow handler addresses cells by `data-date` rather than by `querySelectorAll` index.

**6. Stop refetching the year on every month change.**
- Split the two activity requests into separate effects so the year range depends only on `today` and `attempt`.

**7. Define or drop coverage.**
- State the denominator in the label, or render it as a ratio of real figures in the ledger line. A bold undefined percentage with no action attached does not stay.

**8. Empty month.**
- Use `.empty-state` naming the next action, placed where the month's content would be rather than as a muted line beneath it.

**Verification:** `tests/e2e/calendar.spec.ts` (and any calendar coverage in the timeline specs) is updated for the new grid structure and keyboard model and must pass, with added coverage for the roving tabindex and for each of the three destinations. The stale captures in `docs/design/evidence/calendar/` are retaken at 1440 and 390 in both themes.

## Provisional acceptance criteria

- [ ] At most one control bar and one header separate the page heading from the grid; the month is stated once.
- [ ] Month cells are dense enough that a month reads as a single object, and the grid does not stretch to the full data-page width.
- [ ] Every mark a cell can display appears in the visible legend, and marks come from `icons.tsx` rather than raw glyphs.
- [ ] Days with a diary, days with only trades or reviews, and empty days are visually distinct, and each cell announces its destination to assistive technology and to sighted users.
- [ ] The year view shows a full year without horizontal scrolling at desktop widths, carries month labels, and keeps a focusable scroll region where it must scroll.
- [ ] The year strip encodes levels from the activity counts, its legend states what each level means, and the "371 days" figure is not user-facing copy.
- [ ] The level ramp uses no market-direction colors, so a high-activity day cannot read as a gain or a loss.
- [ ] The month grid is a single tab stop with arrow-key movement, matching the year strip; Home/End behavior is unchanged.
- [ ] Changing month issues one request for that month and does not refetch the year range.
- [ ] Coverage states its denominator or is removed; the unsupported-year warnings and "not computed" states are unchanged.
- [ ] An empty month renders `.empty-state` with a next action.
- [ ] Holiday hatching, timezone handling, US equity calendar semantics and all three destinations behave exactly as before.
- [ ] The page works in all three locales, light and dark themes, keyboard-only, and at 200% zoom with no horizontal page overflow.
- [ ] `docs/design/evidence/calendar/` captures are retaken against the current visual system, with the visual change justified against DESIGN.md.

## Settled during triage

**The year strip stays on this page.** Ruled 2026-10-04: the month grid and the year strip are two views of the same data at two scales, so the apparent overlap with Timeline is not duplication. Step 4 improves the strip in place; moving it is off the table.

**The year strip encodes real levels.** Ruled 2026-10-04. Binary presence wastes the strip's only extra channel over the month grid, and `DiaryActivityDay` already carries `diaryId`, `transactionCount` and `reviewCount`, so the data costs nothing to obtain. Step 4 therefore drops the presence framing and the "Past 371 days" heading in favor of a levelled year view with a legend that states what each level means. The level scale itself — which counts feed it and how many steps — is the remaining implementation detail; keep it to at most four steps so the ramp stays readable, and keep it clear of the market palette per DESIGN.md.
