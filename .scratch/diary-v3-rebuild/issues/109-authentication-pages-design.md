# [109] Design the authentication pages

## Observation

Found during the [whole-app page score](../../../docs/design/ui-page-score-2026-10-06.md).
`/login` and `/register` scored **58/100**, `/forgot-password` **55**, `/reset-password`
**60**, `/register/complete` **63** — the lowest band of any public surface and the only
public pages that look undesigned next to the 2026-10-06 home rebuild, which scored 90.

**The form sits in a void.** At 1440×900 the content occupies a ~376px centred column in
the top 500px of the viewport; the remaining ~320px above the footer is empty, and the
1,064px either side of the column is empty. The column is correctly centred and the fields
are correctly built — visible labels, 44px heights, proper association — but no
composition surrounds them. The page reads as a form that was placed rather than designed.

**The secondary paths are two stacked underlined links.** `/login` offers "Forgot
password?" and "Create account" as identical bare text links on consecutive lines. They
have different weights in reality — one is recovery, one is the main alternative journey —
and the system has a quiet-button idiom for exactly this.

**The pages say nothing about the product.** A guest arriving at `/login` from a shared
link or a bookmark sees a bare form under the header. The home page one click away makes a
specific, well-written argument; none of it reaches the page where people actually decide
to sign up. `/register` in particular is the conversion surface and carries no reason to
convert.

**`/forgot-password` is a dead end in the default configuration.** With optional account
email unconfigured it renders "Email password recovery is not enabled. Contact an
administrator or return to sign in." plus two links. That is honest and correct, but the
page gives no indication *before* you arrive that recovery is unavailable, and `/login`
links to it unconditionally.

**`/register` has no password confirmation and no strength feedback**, while
`/settings/security` — which scored 82 — does have "Confirm new password". The two
password-entry surfaces in the same app have different safeguards for the same risk.

Note the stylesheet-import defect recorded in the
[2026-09-06 layout review](../../../docs/design/ui-consistency-review.md), where a fresh
direct load of `/login` requested no public CSS and the header computed to
`display: block; padding: 0`. That was fixed by moving the import to root, and the current
captures confirm the header renders correctly — this ticket is about composition, not that
regression. Keep the direct-load check in whatever coverage is added.

## Requested outcome

Compose the authentication pages as deliberate public surfaces consistent with the current
home page, without turning them into marketing pages. Preserve every behaviour: the
return-destination handling, error summaries with selectable requestIds, the
account-recovery support URL, and the unconfigured-email states.

## Implementation plan

**1. Decide the composition.** Options, for triage: vertically centre the form and let the
page be deliberately quiet; or use the two-column shape the home hero already establishes,
with the form on one side and a short statement of what the product is on the other. The
second reuses an existing pattern and gives `/register` something to argue with; the first
is cheaper and arguably more appropriate for a return journey. These may differ between
`/login` (return) and `/register` (first contact) — they do not have to match.

**2. Give the secondary paths their real weights.** One alternative journey and one
recovery path should not be two identical links.

**3. Hide or mark recovery when it is unavailable.** If optional account email is not
configured, `/login` should not offer a recovery link that leads to a dead end — or should
mark it. The capability is already discoverable via `/api/auth/capabilities`.

**4. Align the password safeguards.** Decide whether registration gets a confirmation
field, and make `/register` and `/settings/security` agree. Password rules are already
stated in copy ("Use at least 8 characters"), so this is about confirmation and feedback,
not about rule text.

**5. Carry the token-state pages along.** `/reset-password` and `/register/complete` are
the same shape and should get the same composition, including their invalid- and
expired-token states.

**Verification:** existing auth coverage must pass. Add a direct-load check per page
(fresh browser context, no prior navigation) asserting the public shell and its stylesheet
are present at 320, 390 and 1440 — this is what the earlier CSS-import defect broke, and
it is cheap to pin. Assert the return-destination round trip still works from each page.

## Settled during triage

**1. Step 1: the two-column shape, and the same one on all five pages.** The form keeps
its own 440px column; beside it sits a statement of what the product is — one page-specific
sentence over the three-step sequence the home page already makes its argument with, as a
ruled list rather than tiles. Reasons for one composition rather than two: a reader moves
between these five addresses inside one task, and a page shape that changes under them
costs more than the small gain from giving `/login` a quieter treatment. What differs is the
sentence, which is where the difference actually lives — `/register` states what the diary
is, `/login` states that the record is where they left it, and the recovery pages state
that recovery restores access without changing anything written. The surrounding column is
vertically centred rather than top-aligned, which is what closed the empty remainder.

**2. The copy is the product's own, not new marketing.** The three steps are worded as
`home.tsx` words them. A guest arriving at `/register` from a shared link never sees the
home page, so the argument has to reach the page where the decision is made — but these
stay forms first, and nothing on them claims anything the home page does not.

**3. Step 2: weights.** The alternative journey (`Create account` / `Sign in`) is a
secondary button; recovery is a quiet muted link beside it, under a hairline that separates
both from the form's own submit. Two identical underlined links said the two were the same
kind of thing, and they are not: one is the other half of this page's job, the other is a
repair.

**4. Step 3: the recovery link is hidden when no recovery exists in any form — and that
is narrower than the ticket assumed.** The first implementation hid the link whenever
`passwordRecoveryAvailable` was false, and that broke an accepted behaviour:
`ui-ux-audit-regressions.spec.ts` pins that recovery stays discoverable when SMTP is off
**but a support route is configured**, because `/forgot-password` then hands over "Get
sign-in help". With a support route the page is not a dead end, so the link must stay. The
shipped rule is `recoveryPathOffered(capability, supportUrl)`: offered when account email
can send a reset **or** `ACCOUNT_RECOVERY_SUPPORT_URL` is set, and withheld only when
neither exists. The support route is server configuration, so `/login` gained a loader for
it, matching `/forgot-password`. Marking the link instead of hiding it was rejected: a
control that announces it does not work is still a control. While capabilities are
resolving and no support route is configured the link is absent, which is correct —
unknown is not available — and the `/forgot-password` page itself stays reachable by
address either way.

This is worth reading before the next change here: the ticket's own step 3 is only right
in the configuration where nothing is configured, and the test that caught it was written
for an earlier review.

**5. Step 4: registration gets a confirmation field, and the register form still needs
JavaScript.** `/register` was the only password-entry surface in the app without one, while
`/settings/security` and `/register/complete` both have it — and registration is the one
password that may have no recovery path at all when account email is unconfigured. It is
reported on the field and in the error summary, never by disabling the submit, per
DESIGN.md's rule that a disabled button must not stand in for validation. What did **not**
change: `/register` still holds its form behind the capability probe, because rendering a
password field before knowing the mode is email-only would be wrong. So the composition and
the statement render without JavaScript on all five pages — the home page's standard, which
is readability from the first response — while submitting still requires it. That is
pre-existing and out of this ticket's scope; it is stated here rather than quietly dropped.

**6. Step 5: the token pages share the composition unchanged in behaviour.**
`/reset-password`, `/register/complete` and `/forgot-password` wrap their existing state
machines in the same `AuthPage`, including the invalid-token, expired-token,
unconfigured-email and support-URL states.
